## 1. Proposed Subsections

### 5.1 Overall Training Time and Optimization Progression (~1.55 p)
- **Must show:** End-to-end CART and C4.5 training times (depth ≤ 10, unpruned) across all 5 real datasets (7 entries incl. 15M ECBDL'14 and 4M SOREL-20M GPU subsamples) for all 5 backends on `epyc` and `hopper` (H100), plus the external libraries on HIGGS and MNIST8M as the single canonical depth-10 timing reference.
- **Key claims & evidence:**
  - Each algorithmic/architectural step yields large gains: `Serial Simple` → `Serial Optimized` **16–35×** (e.g., HIGGS C4.5 259→7.42 s), → `Multicore CPU` (48T `epyc`) **13.5–40.1×** (peaking at SOREL-20M CART 608→15.1 s), → `GPU-Based` (H100) **1.07–1.71×** (`cluster-{susy,higgs,mnist8m,ecbdl,sorel}/*/summary.csv`).
  - Full ECBDL'14 (32M×631) and SOREL-20M (12.7M×2,381) exceed 80 GiB VRAM and train in 9.2–15.5 s on `epyc`.
  - At depth ≤ 10, `GPU-Based` beats `Hybrid` by 2–60% (e.g., SUSY 154 vs 262 ms, HIGGS 221 vs 329 ms) because `Hybrid`'s 256 MB page-locked hand-off buffers add 0.10–0.11 s of timed setup while shallow trees hand off little work—motivating §5.3.

### 5.2 Comparison with CPU and GPU Libraries: Speed, Phases, Accuracy, and Memory (~1.50 p)
- **Must show:** Head-to-head comparison on HIGGS and MNIST8M against CPU baselines (scikit-learn, rpart, Weka J48, YaDT 1T/48T) and GPU baselines (cuML 128 bins, XGBoost 256 bins, CART only), explaining *why* our exact GPU backend beats binned GPU libraries via phase timelines, and comparing tree fidelity, test accuracy (unpruned d10, d30, and **pruned**), and peak RAM/VRAM.
- **Key claims & evidence:**
  - **CPU baselines** (`bench/cluster-cpu-vs-others/vs-epyc-{higgs,mnist8m}/summary.csv`): on 1 thread, `Serial Optimized` beats scikit-learn by **27.3×** (HIGGS) and **8.8×** (MNIST8M), rpart by **19.2×** (HIGGS; rpart segfaults on MNIST8M's 6.3B values), Weka J48 by **103× / 147×**, and YaDT by **12.9× / 2.3×**. At 48 threads, `Multicore CPU` reaches **312–623×** over scikit-learn, **1,992–4,876×** over J48, and **2.8–48.6×** over 48T YaDT (noting YaDT's non-C4.5 continuous split on HIGGS: 70.09% vs 63.73%).
  - **GPU baselines & phases** (`bench/cluster-gpu-vs-others/summary.csv`): on H100 (d10, same staged 46 GB/s upload), exact `GPU-Based` beats cuML by **2.7× / 2.4×** (0.213 vs 0.584 s; 2.25 vs 5.38 s) and XGBoost by **2.4× / 3.3×** (0.505 s; 7.47 s). Our GPU presort hides inside the upload (starts 7–9 ms after upload, finishes within 1 ms of it), whereas XGBoost spends 2.35 s binning MNIST8M and both libraries have slow per-node histogram builds (4.50–4.56 s vs our 1.43 s).
  - **Deep-tree gap & accuracy** (`vs-hopper-higgs-depth30`): at depth ≤ 30 on HIGGS (2.16M nodes), `Hybrid` (**0.821 s**) beats `GPU-Based` (0.994 s, **1.21×**), cuML (4.18 s, **5.1×**), and XGBoost (23.84 s, **29.0×**). While depth-10 test accuracy agrees within 0.05 pp, at depth 30 binning inflates trees by 8–21% (2.34–2.61M nodes) and drops test accuracy by **0.58 pp** (68.19% vs 68.77% exact). Pruned-tree accuracy (**NEW RUN NEEDED**, see §4) confirms fidelity under realistic pruning and shows depth-10 underfitting (especially C4.5).

### 5.3 Deep Trees: When Hybrid Outperforms GPU-Based (~1.10 p)
- **Must show:** Depth sweep (5→30 and unlimited) across HIGGS, MNIST8M, and SOREL 4M on H100 and A100, concurrency profiling showing *why* `Hybrid` overtakes `GPU-Based`, and hand-off threshold ($m$) behavior.
- **Key claims & evidence:**
  - **Crossover & peak speedup** (`bench/hybrid-vs-gpu-only/BENCHMARK.md`, `*-{hopper,ampere}-{higgs,mnist8m,sorel_4m}/summary.csv`): `GPU-Based` wins up to depth 10; `Hybrid` crosses over at depth 15 (MNIST8M, SOREL 4M) and depth 25 (HIGGS). At unlimited depth on H100, `Hybrid` is **1.28× / 1.76×** faster on HIGGS (CART/C4.5), **1.52× / 1.61×** on MNIST8M, and **1.18× / 1.08×** on SOREL 4M (on A100: **1.75–1.78×**, **1.41–1.46×**, **1.07–1.16×**).
  - **Mechanism (MNIST8M profiling)** (`hybrid-vs-gpu-only/profile/*-hopper-mnist8m/`): concurrent GPU+CPU execution grows from 0% (d5) to **61% (CART) / 74% (C4.5)** of wall time at unlimited depth (177–187 CPU thread-seconds hidden behind GPU levels), cutting `GPU-Based`'s host launch/sync overhead from 14–18% (1.68–2.74 s) to **1% (0.05–0.07 s)**. On narrow HIGGS (no limit), A100's 48 CPU cores actually finish the hybrid build faster than H100's 32 cores (0.65 vs 0.72 s).
  - **Tree-shape limit & $m$ sensitivity** (`hybrid-vs-gpu-only/sweep/`): SOREL 4M gains little (1.08–1.18×) because its unlimited trees are thin and skewed (depth 231–572, 70–88k nodes), leaving large nodes on the GPU. Optimal $m$ scales with width (HIGGS 256–512, MNIST8M 8,192, SOREL 4M 16,384), matching Section 3's default $10F$ rule within ~2%.

### 5.4 Performance Across Hardware and Memory Footprint (~0.95 p)
- **Must show:** All four cluster servers (`epyc`, `dell`, `ampere` A100, `hopper` H100) across all 7 real datasets, plus peak Host RAM and VRAM across all 5 backends.
- **Key claims & evidence:**
  - **CPU vs CPU & GPU vs GPU** (`bench/cluster-{susy,higgs,mnist8m,ecbdl,sorel}/BENCHMARK.md`): `epyc` (DDR5-6400, full channels) outperforms `dell` (8 of 16 channels) by **1.4–1.8×** on 1 thread and **1.4–3.0×** at 48 threads (widest on bandwidth-bound ECBDL'14 2.6–3.0× and SOREL-20M 2.4–2.8×). H100 beats A100 by **1.4–1.5×** on large datasets (MNIST8M, ECBDL'14 15M, SOREL 4M), whereas A100 ties `epyc` 48T on MNIST8M (3.16 vs 3.26 s) and beats H100 slightly on tiny SUSY (142 vs 154 ms, shorter setup).
  - **Memory footprint (RAM & VRAM)**: `Serial Simple` needs ~1× raw data (0.5–118 GiB); presorted CPU backends need ~3× raw data (1.0–350 GiB, ~12 B/value + scratch); `GPU-Based` and `Hybrid` (releasing host raw values after upload and reusing the hand-off arena) need only **~1× raw data in host RAM** (0.7–38 GiB, **3× less RAM** than `Multicore CPU`) plus **~2× raw data in VRAM** (1.2–75.9 GiB, ~8 B/value), explaining why ECBDL'14 15M (73.5 GiB) and SOREL 4M (75.9 GiB) approach the 80 GiB GPU ceiling.

### 5.5 Scalability and Execution Profiling (~1.40 p)
- **Must show:** Multicore thread scaling (1→48T on `epyc` and `dell`), sample scaling and **cost per sample** on Chess $3\times 3$ from $10^6$ to $2\cdot 10^9$ rows, and step-share execution profiling (Chess 10M→2B, HIGGS, ECBDL'14 15M).
- **Key claims & evidence:**
  - **Thread scaling** (`bench/cluster-cpu-scaling/cpu-{epyc,dell}-{higgs,mnist8m}/scaling.csv`): on wide MNIST8M, `epyc` reaches **34.9× CART / 32.8× C4.5** (73% / 68% efficiency) vs `dell`'s **25.9× / 23.5×** (54% / 49%, bandwidth-bound at 12T/24T NUMA boundaries). On narrow HIGGS, `epyc` reaches **22.8× / 18.6×** (47% / 39%) and `dell` **19.1× / 15.9×** (40% / 33%).
  - **Row scaling to $2\cdot 10^9$ & cost per sample** (`bench/cluster-chess/BENCHMARK.md`, `bench/cluster-profiling/BENCHMARK.md`): fixed 17-node depth-4 Chess tree isolates per-sample cost. `epyc` Multicore wins at 1M–10M (15–62 ms) because GPU setup dominates; H100 crosses over between 10M and 100M and scales linearly to **$2\cdot 10^9$ rows in 1.52 s CART / 1.61 s C4.5** (**0.76–0.80 ns/sample**), **2.2–2.5×** faster than `epyc` (3.44 / 3.99 s; 1.72–2.00 ns/sample) and **5.8–6.4×** faster than `dell` (9.02 / 10.36 s). Memory scales linearly (at 2B: 87 GiB RAM on `epyc`, 21 GiB RAM + 53–54 GiB VRAM on H100).
  - **Time breakdown** (`bench/cluster-profiling/`): on CPUs, **Partition** dominates big sets (**38–69%** on `epyc`, 68.9% on ECBDL'14 15M; 36–68% on `dell`), with Presort 20–46% and Split Search 10–25%. On H100, as Chess grows 10M→2B, fixed setup ("Other") drops from **88.5%→6.1%** (`GPU-Based`) and **94.4%→12.0%** (`Hybrid`), while **GPU-busy share** rises from **2–3%→60–64%** (and **74–80%** on ECBDL'14 15M), leaving Partition (45–51%) and Transfer (14–31%) dominant. Briefly note CPU C4.5's bimodal variance on Chess 500M–2B (15–39% spread).

- **Key claims & evidence:**
  - **ECBDL'14 15M, d10** (`bench/cluster-power/ecbdl_15m-depth10/summary.csv`): energy tracks training time across 3 orders of magnitude (685→39.8→2.87→**2.03 kJ** for C4.5); H100 `GPU-Based` saves **27% (CART, 1.98 vs 2.73 kJ)** and **29% (C4.5, 2.03 vs 2.87 kJ)** vs `epyc` Multicore (**2.5–2.6×** above idle: 0.86–0.89 vs 2.16–2.27 kJ). `Hybrid` uses 3–5% more than `GPU-Based` at equal power (~704–709 W).
  - **MNIST8M, no depth limit** (`bench/cluster-power/mnist8m-nodepth/summary.csv`): `Hybrid` is 1.52–1.58× faster than `GPU-Based` at 1.42–1.46× power (994/937 W vs 679/661 W), cutting whole-server energy by **4.0% (CART: 7.85 vs 8.17 kJ)** and **10.5% (C4.5: 8.95 vs 10.0 kJ)** via shorter idle burn (405 W), though using 31–41% more dynamic energy above idle. `Multicore` on `hopper` uses **3.1–3.5×** more energy (26.1 / 31.3 kJ). Note counterfactual excluding the 3 unused H100s (~154 W).

## 5. Anticipated Panel Disagreements

1. **Keeping BOTH `Fig. 1 (test-bars)` and `Tables 1–2` in §5.1:** Another panelist may argue `test-bars` duplicates the top half of Tables 1–2 and suggest dropping "This work" from Tables 1–2 or cutting bar value labels. *My argument:* The author explicitly required Fig. 5 (`test-bars`) AND endorsed the exact style of Tables 1–2; log bars convey 4-order-of-magnitude ratios visually while Tables 1–2 serve as the single numerical anchor (including libraries), provided we eliminate *all* repetition in §5.2–§5.6 (no second depth-10 timing table).
2. **Merging CPU and GPU library comparisons into one subsection (§5.2) vs keeping them separate:** Separate subsections mirror the draft `results.tex` outline, *but* both evaluate only HIGGS and MNIST8M and both share the exact-vs-approximate accuracy and memory story; merging lets one compact Table 3 compare accuracy, nodes, and memory across *all* CPU and GPU libraries side by side.
3. **Plotting Chess in `ns/sample` (Fig. 6a) instead of raw seconds:** Raw log-log seconds hides deviations from linear scaling (e.g., `Serial`'s cache-spill slowdown at 1B and where GPU setup finishes amortizing). `ns/sample` turns linear scaling into a flat horizontal asymptote while annotating raw seconds (`1.52 s` at 2B) directly on the curves.
4. **Showing CART-only in the dense Hardware heatmap (Fig. 4a) and Profiling stacked bars (Fig. 6b):** Showing both CART and C4.5 doubles figure height (~+0.8 p) while the two algorithms' relative hardware ratios and step shares match within ~5–10% across datasets (the sole notable C4.5 profiling quirk—CPU Chess "Other" 10–15%—is stated in prose/caption).
5. **Omitting a dedicated `$m$` hand-off sensitivity figure:** The sweep curves (`hybrid-vs-gpu-only/sweep/`) are on an older untimed-setup commit (`f53347a9`) with missing SOREL points (`1024, 2048`), and the author already treats tuned $m$ as a standing design choice; devoting 0.35 p to an older-commit U-curve is poor value compared to Fig. 3b's concurrency breakdown.
